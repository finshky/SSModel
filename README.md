# SSModel

**SSModel** is a Mathematica package for computing gravitational-wave (GW) spectra sourced by acoustic oscillations following cosmological first-order phase transitions, based on the **sound-shell model**.

The package implements the framework described in [arXiv:1909.10040](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.10040) and [arXiv:2308.12943](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.12943).

It also includes **`SSModel_2f.wl`**, an extension to a two-fluid scenario in which sound waves generated by a phase transition in a dark sector can transfer energy into the visible sector through weak interactions between the two fluids.

## Package structure

```text
SSModel/
├── SSModelBubbleFluidProfiles.wl
├── SSModel.wl
```

## Main functions

### `Profile`

Computes the fluid profiles around expanding phase-transition bubbles, including:

- Fluid velocity
- Enthalpy
- Energy-density perturbations

### `GetKineticSpecrum`

Computes the dimensionless kinetic spectrum, $\zeta_\mathrm{kin}(K)$, following Eqs. (33) and (41) of [arXiv:2308.12943](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.12943).

If the requested spectrum is not already available in `data/kinetic.wl`, it is computed and stored for subsequent use.

### `OmegaGW`

Computes the GW spectrum,

$$
\Omega_\mathrm{GW}(\delta\tau_\mathrm{fin},k) = 3 \frac{\bar w_d^2}{\bar{\rho}^2}  k^3 {\cal T}_{\rm GW} 
\int_{-1}^1 \left(1 - z^2 \right)^2 dz 
    \int_0^\infty  dp
        \frac{p^2}{\tilde p^4} \, E_\mathrm{kin} (p)
        E_\mathrm{kin} (\tilde p) 
        \Delta (\delta\tau_\mathrm{fin}, k, p, \tilde p)
$$

following Eq. (51) of [arXiv:2308.12943](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.12943).

### `OmegaGW2f`

Computes the gravitational-wave spectrum in the two-fluid scenario, following Eq. (45) of arXiv xx.yy. This introduces the factor $F^2(\tau)$ in the time integral $\Delta$ (see **Time integrals** below).

The two-fluid extension introduces two additional parameters:

- **Interaction rate:** $\gamma \equiv \frac{\Gamma_\mathrm{int}}{H_\ast}$, where $\Gamma\_{\mathrm{int}}$ is the interaction rate between the fluids and $H_\ast$ is the Hubble parameter at the phase transition.
- **Enthalpy ratio:** $r_w \equiv \frac{\bar{w}\_v}{\bar{w}\_d}$, where $\bar{w}\_v$ and $\bar{w}\_d$ are the background enthalpy densities of the visible and dark fluids, respectively.

## Time integrals

The GW spectra depend on the time integrals (Eq. 46 of arXiv xx.yy)

$$
\Delta (\delta\tau_\mathrm{fin},  k, p, \tilde p) \equiv 
\int_{\tau_0}^{\tau_\ast} \,\frac{d \tau_1}{\tau_1} 
\int_{\tau_0}^{\tau_\ast} \, \frac{d \tau_2}{\tau_2}
\cos (p c_s \tau_-)
\cos (\tilde p c_s \tau_-)
\cos (k\tau_-)  F(\tau_1) \, F(\tau_2)\,
$$

where (Eq. 44)

$$
F(\tau) = \frac{1}{1 + r_w} \left[ 1 + r_w e^{-2\Gamma_\mathrm{int}(\tau - \tau_\ast)} \right] .
$$

Defining $\hat{p}_{mn} \equiv k+c_s(m p + n \tilde p)$ and using trigonometric identities (see Sec. IV.A of [2308.12943](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.12943)), the integral can be written as

$$
\Delta (\delta\tau_\mathrm{fin},  k, p, \tilde p) = \sum_{m,n=\pm 1} \Delta_{mn}(\delta\tau_\mathrm{fin}, \hat{p}_{mn}) ,
$$

where

$$
\Delta_{mn} (\delta\tau_\mathrm{fin}, \hat{p}_{mn}) = 
\frac{1}{4(1+r_w)^2} 
\left\lvert
    \Delta Ei(\delta\tau_\mathrm{fin}, \hat{p}_{mn}) + 
    r_w \Delta \tilde E_1(\delta\tau_\mathrm{fin}, \hat{p}_{mn}) 
\right\rvert^2
$$

and we defined the exponential integrals

$$
\begin{align}
Ei(\tau, \hat{p}_{mn}) &= \int \frac{d\tau}{\tau} e^{i \hat{p}_{mn}\tau} \\
\tilde E_1(\tau, \hat{p}_{mn}) &= e^\frac{2\Gamma_\mathrm{int}}{H_\ast}\int \frac{d\tau}{\tau} e^{-(2\Gamma_\mathrm{int}-i\hat{p}_{mn}) \tau} \\
\Delta f(\delta\tau_\mathrm{fin}, \hat{p}_{mn}) &\equiv f(\tau_\mathrm{fin}) - f(\tau_\ast) \\
\end{align}
$$

These are implemented in `SSModel` via the built-in functions `ExpIntegralEi` and `ExpIntegralE`.

Notice that for a transition in a secluded sector, where $\Gamma_\mathrm{int}=0$, the expression above reduces to Eq. 56 of [2308.12943](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.12943), since $\Delta \tilde E_1\rvert_{\Gamma_\mathrm{int}=0} = \Delta Ei$:

$$
\begin{align}
\Delta_{mn} (\delta\tau_\mathrm{fin}, \hat{p}_{mn})\rvert_{\Gamma_\mathrm{int}=0} 
&= \frac{1}{4} 
    \left\lvert
        \Delta Ei(\delta\tau_\mathrm{fin}, \hat{p}_{mn})  
    \right\rvert^2 \\
&= \frac{1}{4} 
    \left[\Delta Ci^2(\delta\tau_\mathrm{fin}, \hat{p}_{mn}) + \Delta Si^2(\delta\tau_\mathrm{fin}, \hat{p}_{mn})\right] .
\end{align}
$$

$Ci$ and $Si$ are the real and imaginary parts of $Ei$.

## Numerical performance and Python implementation

The Mathematica implementation can be computationally expensive in certain parameter regimes. In particular, numerical integrations performed using Mathematica's `NIntegrate` may take a considerable amount of time to converge.

To provide an alternative for these cases, this repository also includes the Python script **GW_models_2f.wl**, Python script extending [`cosmoGW`](https://github.com/) to the two-fluid scenario. This implementation provides an additional route for computing the gravitational-wave spectrum when the Mathematica calculations become prohibitively slow.
